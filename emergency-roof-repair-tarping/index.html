Insurance — plain English

Will insurance cover this?

Sudden damage — hail, wind, a fallen tree — is usually covered by Texas homeowners insurance. Wear, age and slow leaks usually are not. And a denied claim still sits on your record, so a small repair is often not worth filing for. If your damage came from a storm, see our storm & hail damage page for the full plain-English guide, including deductibles.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

How much does emergency roof leak repair / tarping cost?

Emergency after-hours leak repair in Houston runs about $400–$2,000 (RISE, Mar 2026). Tarping ranges from $150 to $1,500 across sources — no single tarping price is quoted here, because the sources disagree that widely. Your price comes from a free inspection and a written estimate.

Is tarping a permanent fix?

No. A tarp is temporary protection — it stops more water getting in while the real repair is planned. It is not a roof, and it should not be left on for months.

What do I do while I wait for the roofer?

Put buckets under the drip, move valuables and furniture out of the way, and take photos of the damage for your records. Do not climb on a wet roof, and do not make permanent repairs before your insurer has seen storm damage.

Do you come out at night or in the rain?

Emergency tarping is available 24/7 — call first and we will tell you honestly when we can get there. Some work cannot be done safely in heavy rain or lightning, and we will not pretend otherwise.

Will insurance cover emergency tarping?

Temporary repairs to stop more damage are normally part of a storm claim — but check your policy, keep receipts, and photograph everything before and after the tarp goes on.

What counts as a roofing emergency?

Water coming in, a hole in the roof, a limb through the roof, or a section blown off. A small drip that only shows after rain is urgent — but it is not an emergency. Call either way and we will tell you honestly which one it is.
