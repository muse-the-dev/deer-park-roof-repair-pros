Insurance — plain English

Will insurance cover roof damage in Texas?

Usually covered

Sudden damage from a named event — hail that breaks shingles, wind that tears shingles off, a fallen tree. Texas homeowners insurance covers sudden and accidental damage like that.

Usually not covered

Age, wear and tear, and slow leaks. Shingles that reached the end of their life, a pipe boot that cracked slowly in the Houston sun, or a small leak that ran for months and rotted the deck — those are normally the homeowner's cost.

  • Your deductible matters most. Most Texas policies have a separate wind/hail deductible of 1–2% of your home's insured value. On a $350,000 home, a 2% deductible is $7,000 out of pocket — if the repair costs less than that, a claim pays nothing. Compare first.
  • Do not file for a small repair. A denied claim still sits on your record. A $225 pipe boot is not worth a claim.
  • Never let a roofer "take care of" your deductible. A contractor waiving, paying or absorbing any part of your deductible is an offence under Texas Business & Commerce Code §27.02. Any roofer offering that is asking you to join insurance fraud.
  • After a storm, document first. Photograph the roof, ceiling and belongings, and do not make permanent repairs before the insurer has seen the damage.

General information only, based on Texas Department of Insurance guidance via Atrium Roofing and the JC&C cost guide — not legal advice. Your policy wording decides what is covered.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

Does homeowners insurance cover hail damage in Texas?

Usually, yes — hail and wind are sudden, named events, and Texas policies normally cover sudden damage. Wear, age and slow leaks are not covered. Your policy wording decides, and your deductible decides whether a claim pays anything at all.

How soon after a storm should I get my roof checked?

As soon as it is safe. Hail damage is easiest to document while it is fresh, and most policies have deadlines for reporting. Do not make permanent repairs before the insurer has seen the damage — temporary tarping is fine.

Will insurance pay for a full roof replacement, or only repair?

It depends on the damage and your policy. Some policies pay replacement cost, others pay actual cash value — which falls with the roof's age. A 20-year-old roof can pay nothing after the deductible. Get the inspection and the photos first, then compare the numbers.

What is my wind/hail deductible?

Most Texas policies have a separate wind/hail deductible of 1–2% of your home's insured value. On a $350,000 home, 2% is $7,000 out of pocket. If the repair costs less than your deductible, a claim pays nothing.

How do I know if it is hail damage or just wear?

Hail leaves bruises, dents and missing granules in a random pattern, often with dented vents and gutters. Wear is even, gradual granule loss. We photograph both and tell you which one we see — honestly, because a denied claim still sits on your record.

Do I need a full replacement or just a repair?

It depends on how widespread the damage is. Localised hail damage on a roof under 15 years old is usually a repair. Widespread damage, or a roof over 20 years old, may be a replacement — and we will tell you which one it is, in writing, before you spend anything.
